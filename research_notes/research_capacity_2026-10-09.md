# Capacity, allocation and sizing when a gainer strategy has more signals than slots

Research date: 2026-10-09. Scope: questions 1-6 from the brief. Excludes the topics already covered (pump-and-dump detection, MAX momentum, Moura et al. attention timing, OI squeeze case studies).

**Evidence quality overall:** weak to moderate. No academic or practitioner source tests "replace a held position with a new #1 24h gainer" directly. Most of what follows comes from nearby fields: equity and ETF momentum rotation, managed-futures portfolio construction, and Kelly theory. Where a claim rests only on a backtest or comes from a vendor, it is flagged.

---

## 1. Capacity: when should a new signal displace an existing position?

**What practitioners do.** Rank-based rotation systems almost always add a *hysteresis buffer*: a held name is replaced only when it falls well outside the entry rank, or when the challenger beats it by a clear margin. A replacement at the first sign of a better candidate is rare.
- CXO Advisory tested a "Buffered Winner" rule on asset-class ETF momentum: keep the past winner until a new winner beats it by 4-5%. It beat plain winner-switching, but **most of the outperformance came from one year (2010)**. This is in-sample with a tiny universe ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/momentum-investing/buffered-winner-asset-class-etf-momentum-strategy/)).
- A practitioner rule for a 10-stock momentum portfolio replaces a holding only when it drops below rank 20, not rank 11. This is anecdotal ([thechartist.com.au](https://thechartist.com.au/?p=101844)).
- Calluzzo, Moneta and Topaloglu (2025) report that momentum's turnover is the main obstacle to collecting its returns. Their fixes are to filter out names that are about to leave the portfolio and to blend rankings across lookbacks, both of which cut turnover ([Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/momentum-investing/)).
- Robert Carver's "dynamic optimisation" for small accounts runs a greedy search for the integer portfolio closest to the ideal fractional one. A shadow cost on trading stops it from churning. In a $100k backtest it held only about 6 of 45 instruments at a time, and its Sharpe ratio (1.19) was close to the unconstrained ideal (1.17). **Carver himself says the first version "didn't work as well as hoped" because costs were wrong for sparse positions. The results are full-sample backtests with no true out-of-sample test** ([qoppac](https://qoppac.blogspot.com/2021/10/mr-greedy-and-tale-of-minimum-tracking.html)).

**Time stops and "dead money".** I found no study that tests a time stop against a rank-based exit. Older trend-following work finds that the trend reversal itself is the best stop, and that extra stop-loss layers add little ([CXO S&P trend study](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/following-sp-500-index-trends)). Momentum holding-period studies cut both ways. Crypto rotation profits fall quickly as the holding period lengthens ([Arch](https://blog.arch.finance/momentum-investing-strategy-and-the-crypto-market), vendor backtest). Others argue that the "momentum decays" pattern is partly a rebalancing artefact ([Moontower](https://moontowermeta.com/how-much-of-momentum-is-caused-by-randomness/)).

**Takeaway for this bot.** Your backtests rejected "replace oldest / worst / losing". That fits the literature: unconditional replacement is turnover, and turnover destroys momentum P&L. A replacement rule that might survive is **margin-gated**: the incumbent must be clearly stale, and the challenger clearly stronger, on a measure that is *not* a simple entry feature (your entry-feature AUCs were about 0.5). The cleanest such measure is the incumbent's own state, such as no new high for X hours or rank decay. A better challenger score is not that measure, because you have already shown you cannot score challengers.

## 2. Risk-based vs notional exposure ("free trade" positions)

**The concept is standard.** Seykota and Druz's "portfolio heat" is the sum, across all positions, of (price - stop) x size. Practitioners cap heat (Van Tharp's example runs about 6% of equity; Wealth-Lab quotes 20-25% as a common maximum) rather than notional ([Wealth-Lab](https://www2.wealth-lab.com/WL5Wiki/psPortfolioHeat.ashx), [Van Tharp 2006](https://vantharp.com/Weekly_update/Weekly_284_Aug_16_2006.htm)). A position whose stop sits at or above entry has zero *planned* heat. Freeing slots on that basis is therefore internally consistent, and it is how many CTAs think: Auspice shows one position that was 55% of notional but only 4% of VaR ([Auspice](https://www.auspicecapital.com/alt-invest/2021/12/2/using-leverage-to-reduce-risk)).

**Pitfalls, ranked for this bot:**
1. **Gap and slippage through the stop.** On 10 Oct 2025, top-of-book depth on major venues fell more than 90%, spreads reached double-digit percentages, and some altcoins wicked 70-80% or printed near zero. A "risk-free" altcoin long with a stop at entry could have filled far below it ([FTI Consulting](https://fticonsulting.com/insights/articles/crypto-crash-october-2025-leverage-met-liquidity)). Your coins are the most illiquid #1 gainers, so their realistic worst case is a slip of 10-30% beyond the stop, not zero.
2. **Correlation spikes.** Pumped altcoins dump together in market-wide deleveraging. The rule of thumb n_eff = n / (1+(n-1)rho), with rho of 0.5-0.8 for small caps in stress, means 4 "free" positions behave like roughly 1.3-1.6 independent bets ([LuxAlgo Kelly note](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/kelly-criterion/), heuristic). Planned heat understates the real loss in exactly the scenario that hurts.
3. **Breakeven stops cost expectancy.** Mechanical breakeven moves often reduce average profit per trade because pullbacks "scratch" trades that would have worked ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/breakeven-move-rules/), with no cited study). This matters because "risk-free" status comes from your +10% lock. The 2026 microstructure paper (section 5) shows strong 15-minute reversal after large taker-driven moves, which raises scratch risk for a stop placed exactly at entry.
4. **Margin and liquidation on perpetuals.** In cross mode, a locked-in winner still consumes initial margin and shares one margin balance with everything else. A "free" position does not free margin; it frees *risk budget*. Those are separate constraints and need separate accounting.

**Verdict:** counting locked positions at *stressed* heat, not zero, is defensible. A conservative version charges each locked position (entry - stop) + 15% x notional as heat.

## 3. Sizing across a variable number of concurrent bets

- **Kelly with simultaneous bets is a joint problem.** Maximise E[log(1+sum f_i X_i)] over all joint outcomes. The single-bet fractions cannot simply be added. For M identical independent bets the problem reduces to one variable, and the probability that all M lose in a turn is (1-p)^M ([arXiv 0803.1364](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0803.1364), [quantvault](https://quantvault.org/problem-439-kelly-optimal-allocation-across-independent-bets.html)). MacLean et al. show that fractional Kelly is preferable over medium and short horizons ([AUEB thesis](https://pyxida.aueb.gr/server/api/core/bitstreams/5b11b70b-f4fd-4894-860c-c17a2b8a847a/content)).
- **Practical implication.** Your edge per gainer trade is a heavy right tail with a low hit rate, and parameter uncertainty is large (AUC about 0.5 means you cannot separate trades). That argues for equal sizing within the sleeve plus a sleeve-level total cap, with no per-trade confidence weighting. The lack of evidence for confidence-weighted sizing is consistent with your null feature results.
- **Volatility-scaled sizing.** Zarattini, Pagani and Barbon's crypto trend paper (top 20 liquid coins, ensemble Donchian) uses volatility-based allocation and reports Sharpe above 1.5 net of fees ([Concretum](https://concretumgroup.com/catching-crypto-trends-a-tactical-approach-for-bitcoin-and-altcoins/)). **The summary page gives no out-of-sample split.** Han, Kang and Ryu find crypto time-series momentum robust but cross-sectional momentum weak once realistic costs and intraday liquidations are applied ([econpapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:eee:ecofin:v:57:y:2021:i:c:s1062940821000590)). For #1 gainers, realised volatility at entry is extreme and varies a lot between coins. Equal-*risk* sizing (size = risk budget / stop distance, with the stop adapted to volatility) is the standard alternative to fixed 20% notional. It is untested here.
- **Anti-martingale scaling** (adding to winners) is the CTA norm, since exposure builds as a trend strengthens ([AlphaSimplex, Kaminski & Yang](https://www.alphasimplex.com/assets/files/risk-variation-in-trend-following-systems_kaminski-and-yang---2020.02-1712610317.pdf)). You already tested pyramiding and it lost, so I do not recommend it again.

## 4. Using cross margin or unrealized profit on Binance

- In Multi-Assets mode (cross only; isolated margin is not allowed), Binance's own formula is `uniAvailableForOrder = Account Equity - sum(asset cross IM x askRate)`, and Account Equity includes unrealized PnL. **Unrealized profit therefore does raise order capacity, and unrealized losses cut it, down to zero** ([Binance margin-ratio FAQ](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/how-to-calculate-margin-ratio-in-the-multi-assets-mode-c983a9268cfa4e70a4916c276bb3b7bc)). Non-stablecoin collateral carries haircuts (2.5-5% for BTC/ETH/BNB; the FAQs disagree) ([supported assets FAQ](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/what-is-multi-assets-mode-and-which-assets-are-supported-29b45c485d664028b9ca1cdf90b24f6f?hl=en), [MA FAQ](https://www.binance.com/en/support/faq/frequently-asked-questions-on-the-multi-assets-mode-f80415bea8124f6494566f23c0bf9c38?hl=en)). Auto-exchange triggers on a negative balance.
- For single-asset cross mode, the API documents `availableBalance` and `totalCrossUnPnl` separately but gives no formula ([Binance API](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/account/rest-api/Account-Information-V3)). Other exchanges (KuCoin, BingX) define available balance as wallet + cross uPnL - IM - order margin ([KuCoin](https://www.kucoin.com/announcement/bd-announcement-on-optimizing-the-calculation-of-cross-margin-isolated-margin-available-balance-and-futures-account-transferable-amount-12-26)). **Verify this empirically** by logging `availableBalance` before and after a winner moves.
- **Risk.** Funding new entries from uPnL is implicit pyramiding at the portfolio level, and it reflexively raises leverage after gains. All cross positions share one margin balance, so a gap on one coin (see 10 Oct 2025) drains margin for every other position, and the exchange can liquidate them together. ADL adds tail risk on the profitable side. For a bot sized at 2x maximum leverage with 30% stops, the *capacity* exists without uPnL. The binding constraint is your own exposure cap, not exchange margin. **Recommendation: do not size from uPnL. Size from equity (which already includes uPnL) and cap gross exposure.**

## 5. New (2025-2026) evidence on gainer continuation and entry timing

- **Short-horizon reversal on Binance perpetuals (arXiv 2608.21888, 2026).** The sample is 2025-01 to 2026-02, with a frozen 6-month holdout to 2026-08. Next-bar reversal is strongest at 15 minutes and gone by 4 hours. It **grows with the size of the prior move** (sign-flip rate 50.2% to 53.0% across move deciles) and concentrates after aggressive taker-driven moves. It replicates in full on Binance USDT-M perpetuals and survives the holdout in weakened form (out-of-sample AUC about 0.53). Brand-new microcaps showed no signal. **Implication:** an order that is market-bought the instant a coin becomes #1 is probably buying the top of a taker burst. A short delay, or a limit at a small pullback, should improve the average fill. The effect is small (gross edge about 1 bp per bar for the generic signal), but conditioned on top-decile moves it may matter more ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2608.21888v1)). This is the only out-of-sample-tested 2025-26 result I found that bears on entry timing.
- **A 2026 microstructure paper argues the opposite for trend execution.** Passive or limit entry "re-labels" slippage as opportunity loss, because the trades that never pull back are the runners ([arXiv 2607.01550](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.01550)). The two papers conflict, so test both directions.
- **"The Quarter-Hour Effect" (arXiv 2607.09426, 2026)** reports periodic algorithmic flow at :00/:15/:30/:45 in crypto futures. This is a possible timing confound for 5-minute entries; I did not verify the details ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.09426v2)).
- **No study found** on "still #1 after N hours", "break of first high" or "second leg" for crypto top gainers. The only related item is CryptoRank's heuristic that a coin appearing on the gainers list across 1h, 24h and 7d indicates persistence ([CryptoRank](https://cryptorank.io/gainers), not evidence). Your own data is the only valid test.

## 6. Rule proposals, ranked by expected value x testability

Each rule must beat the current baseline in **both** year 1 and year 2 separately, with parameters fixed from year 1. Report the median, not the mean, of 1000 bootstrap reorderings of leader arrival, because runner P&L is dominated by a handful of trades.

**R1. Stale-incumbent swap (margin-gated replacement). Highest EV, easy to test.**
(a) Take the new leader when full *only if* some held gainer position is "stale": open for 12 hours or more, still below its +10% lock, and with no new high since entry in the last 6 hours. (b) Allocate the standard 20%. (c) Sell the stalest qualifying position (longest since its high). Grid: age {6, 12, 24}h x no-new-high window {3, 6, 12}h.
*Why it differs from what you tested:* "replace oldest / worst / losing" fires on any incumbent. This rule fires only on incumbents that have already shown the 48h-time-stop failure pattern early, so it is effectively a shorter, conditional time stop. It is backed by the buffer and hysteresis evidence in section 1.

**R2. Shorter conditional time stop (no replacement logic). High EV, trivial to test.**
Close a position that has not touched +10% after T hours, with T in {12, 18, 24, 36}, versus the current 48. This frees slots passively. If R1 wins but R2 alone wins as well, prefer R2, which is simpler. Measure the gain from freed-slot trades separately from the change in P&L on the stopped trades.

**R3. Heat-based third slot ("locked" positions count at stressed risk).**
(a) Allow a 3rd gainer entry when total gainer heat < H, where heat = sum[(price - stop) x qty] + 0.15 x notional per locked position, and H in {15%, 20%, 25%} of equity. (b) Size it at 20%, or 10% as a variant. (c) Sell nothing. Hard cap on gross gainer notional: 60%.
Medium EV. It differs from "more fixed slots" (which failed) because extra capacity appears only after an earlier trade has proven itself. Its risk is correlated gap loss, so report worst-day and worst-week drawdown, and replay the 10 Oct 2025 window explicitly.

**R4. Delayed or pullback entry for every leader.** Enter at the first 5-minute close after T in {15, 30, 60} min from becoming #1, *or* by limit at -3% / -5% from the #1 trigger price with a 2-hour expiry (unfilled orders are skipped). This is backed by the 15-minute reversal evidence. It also acts as a capacity filter, because leaders that never pull back are not entered, which tests the opposing paper's warning directly. Medium EV, easy to test.

**R5. Confirmation gate for overflow leaders only.** When full, record the new leader. If after N in {2, 4, 8}h it is still in the top 3 gainers *and* above its #1 trigger price, enter at 10% via R1-style displacement. This targets exactly your missed runners. Your feature nulls suggest it may not beat chance, so test it after R1 and R2. Lower EV.

**R6. Equal-risk sizing.** Size = 2% equity risk / stop distance, where stop = max(15%, 2.5 x ATR(1h, 24) / price), capped at 20% notional. This lets a volatile leader take a smaller slot, so 3 can fit in 40%. Lower EV: it changes the risk shape more than the capacity.

**Not recommended:** funding entries from unrealized PnL (section 4), confidence-weighted sizing (no separable signal), and further pyramiding or re-entry (already rejected).

**Overfitting guard:** the grids above total about 60 configurations. Apply a deflated-Sharpe correction or require a positive result in both years for at least 70% of each rule's grid cells. Isolated best cells should not count.
