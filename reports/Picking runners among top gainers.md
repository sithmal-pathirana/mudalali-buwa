# Look at positioning, not the price move

No published study, vendor report or audited practitioner record shows out of sample that anything observable when a coin becomes the #1 24h gainer separates the coins that keep running from the ones that fade. The user's own result on 2,533 events (AUC near 0.5, with every feature raising the odds of a big run and of a crash together) matches the research rather than contradicting it. The research finds that crypto features predict the **size** of the next move, not its **direction**. Out-of-sample evidence exists only for neighbouring problems: picking which coin will be pumped (AUC above 0.9), detecting a pump within seconds (94.5% F1), and very small multi-hour order-imbalance effects (R² below 0.2%). Only one published result separates pumps that last from pumps that reverse: in pump-and-dumps, coordinated attention arrives **before** the price peak, while in sustained moves it arrives **after**. That result is small, in-sample, and based on Telegram data the user cannot get for free. The useful opening is in data rather than method. The user has tested almost every feature that can be built from price, volume, funding and trade-tape data. **Positioning data has not been tested**: 5-minute open interest, top-trader and retail long/short ratios (Binance `metrics` files), and 30-second order-book depth (`bookDepth`). Both are free in bulk for the whole 2024–2026 window. The documented mega-runners (MYX, COAI, ALPACA, HIFI, STO) were all squeezes of a cornered float against perp open interest that was large relative to that float. So the most promising new experiments measure **who is trapped and how much fuel is left**, not how the price has moved. Experiments that change the payoff structure, rather than the selection, should run alongside.

## Tests that failed for the user also fail in the published research

Most of the relevant research is on pump-and-dumps, and it answers a different question. La Morgia et al. flag a Binance pump within 25 seconds at **94.5% F1**, and "rush orders" are by far the most important feature (Gini 0.251). The pumps in that study are short: classic Telegram pumps last about five minutes and then "return to [their] natural level" ([arXiv 2105.00733](https://arxiv.org/html/2105.00733v2)). Xu and Livshits predict *which* coin will be pumped with **AUC above 0.9 out of sample** ([USENIX](https://www.usenix.org/conference/usenixsecurity19/presentation/xu-jiahua)), but they never test how long the pump lasts. A coin that reaches #1 on Binance futures has already outlived a typical scheduled pump. So these detectors mostly describe events that never reach the user's trigger. The user's own failed rush-order test fits this: rush orders mark coordinated buying, and coordinated buying comes before runners and before collapses alike.

Cross-sectional anomaly research is no more helpful. Crypto shows "MAX momentum": coins with extreme past daily gains beat low-MAX coins by **1.99–3.03% per week** ([Bilkent](https://repository.bilkent.edu.tr/items/78167f5c-d3e2-4f07-b4bc-5e8a3ed0b2c0)). Grobys and Junttila find the opposite sign, at more than 1.5% per week ([Univ. of Vaasa](https://www.uwasa.fi/en/newshub/news/new-study-detects-lottery-behavior-cryptocurrency-markets)). Short-horizon reversal is strongest in small, illiquid coins ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/apeclt/v28y2021i11p887-893.html)), and momentum is crash-prone enough that a single coin can make it insignificant ([Springer 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-025-00474-9)). Higher skewness predicts *lower* returns ([IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finana/v96y2024ipas1057521924005581.html)). These are weekly mean effects of a few percent, with unstable signs. They are far too weak to separate a +50% three-day outcome from a −30% one. The machine-learning research shows the same pattern. Order-flow models forecast **volatility** well (a CNN cut BTC volatility forecast error from 1.4 to 0.85 RMSPE ([arXiv 2304.02472](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.02472))). For direction, they work only at tick horizons. A model whose features raise both tails, as the user's did, is detecting variance. That is useful for **position sizing and stop width**, but it does not identify which coin will run.

### The only discriminator found: does attention come before or after the peak

Moura et al. matched 20 million Telegram messages to market data. In 47 pump-and-dump events, the median message came **4.3 minutes before** the price peak. In 73 sustained moves, discussion **followed** the peak by a median **14.5 minutes** (KS p = 0.0006). The *text* of the messages could not tell the two apart ([arXiv 2609.01176](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.01176)). Sustained moves happened in more liquid coins, rose further (+18.6% versus +10.0%) and gave back much less (−5.4% versus −14.8%). Two caveats apply. The study split the groups using after-the-fact reversal and volume-decay ratios, so part of the volume difference is built into the definition. The sample is also small and was not tested out of sample. The idea still transfers. Pre-positioned money leaves through the late buyers it attracts. Organic moves keep pulling in new participants after the high. Free futures data can measure an equivalent of this timing, as the next section shows. Industry counts point the same way: about 80–89% of 2024–2025 Binance spot listings fell after listing ([BeInCrypto](https://beincrypto.com/binance-listed-tokens-negative-return)). In this market, news that arrives as expected tends to be sold.

## The documented mega-runners were squeezes of a cornered float

A different picture appears when you look at the actual +300% to +1,000% runners of 2025–2026 rather than at average effects. Every well-documented case combined a **small or cornered float**, **perp open interest large relative to that float**, and **short liquidations**. The trigger was either engineered or forced by the exchange.

| Runner | What drove it | What was visible during the run | What ended it |
|---|---|---|---|
| MYX (Sep 2025) | Under 20% of supply circulating; Bubblemaps found 100 linked wallets ([Cointelegraph](https://cointelegraph.com/news/myx-finance-trading-activity-market-manipulation-report)) | +1,132% Sep 1–8; $11M of short liquidations; perp turnover $6–9B/day; Binance moved funding to hourly ([Wu Blockchain](https://wublock.substack.com/p/the-myx-affair-a-deliberately-orchestrated)) | Peak coincided with a 3.9%-of-supply unlock |
| COAI (Sep–Oct 2025) | About 20% circulating; 60 wallets funded identically ([CoinCentral](https://coincentral.com/bubblemaps-investigates-chainopera-and-claims-one-entity-controls-coai-profits/)) | About $0.14 to about $44.90 ([99Bitcoins](https://99bitcoins.com/news/altcoins/coai-crypto-drops-after-sprinting-100x-is-chain-opera-ai-run-finished/)) | −52% in 24h once the wallet cluster became public |
| ALPACA (Apr 2025) | Binance delisting announced | **OI several times market cap**; about 12x in 3 days; hourly funding capped at ±2% ([TechFlow](https://www.techflowpost.com/en-US/article/25364)) | Futures close on a fixed date |
| HIFI (Sep 2025) | Binance delisting | $6B of derivatives volume driven by short liquidations; delisted peers BAKE and SLF fell 20–25% instead ([Invezz](https://invezz.com/news/2025/09/10/binance-delisting-sparks-volatile-rallies-in-bakerytoken-hifi-finance-and-self-chain/)) | Delisting date |
| STO (Apr 2026) | **One wallet withdrew over 11% of circulating supply** from Binance | OI +300% to $332M; derivatives volume +500% ([altFINS](https://altfins.com/crypto-news/crypto-news-summary/261844)) | Unlock scheduled around the rally |

The two delisting cases are the closest thing to a natural experiment. ALPACA and HIFI squeezed, while BAKE and SLF, which got the same announcement, did not. So the announcement alone did not decide the outcome; the positioning around it did. A practitioner screener for squeeze-prone Binance perps uses the same variables. It treats **OI / circulating market cap above 0.5 as a warning and above 1.0 as danger**, and also scores perp/spot volume ratio, funding APR, listing age under 60–180 days and circulating cap under $30–100M. The author reports that squeeze post-mortems "almost always screened hot" but publishes no backtest ([HackerNoon](https://hackernoon.com/that-300percent-funding-apr-is-not-free-money-screening-for-squeeze-traps-on-binance-perpetuals)). Two cautions follow. First, this is case evidence chosen after the fact. Many coins with high OI relative to market cap surely faded, and nobody has counted them. Second, a cornered float looks like a necessary condition for a 10x, not a sufficient one. That is why it has to be tested against the user's 2,533 events rather than assumed.

The same cases point clearly to what **ends** a run, and that evidence is stronger than anything on entries. Scheduled unlocks (MYX, STO), the futures-close date after a delisting (ALPACA, HIFI), and insider deposits to exchanges mark the tops. Public exposure of a wallet cluster came at or after the top, never before the run. Binance switching a symbol to **hourly funding** came *early* in the ALPACA and MYX runs, not at the top. It is a timestamped sign that the exchange itself sees an extreme squeeze. The low-float underperformance studies (median −81% one-year return for tokens with FDV above $1B ([KuCoin/GSR](https://www.kucoin.com/news/flash/gsr-study-low-circulation-high-fdv-token-models-underperform-by-80-in-one-year))) measure months after launch. They say nothing about the next three days.

## The free 2024–2026 positioning archive is the main untested source

Research on open interest, liquidations, the basis and long/short ratios is almost entirely practitioner opinion or BTC/ETH-only. The usual reading ("price up with OI up means new longs, with OI down means short covering") has **no out-of-sample test on altcoins** ([Mudrex](https://mudrex.com/learn/how-open-interest-impacts-crypto-futures/)). Real cases mix the two: ADA rallied as a "short squeeze" while OI rose 25% ([CryptoTimes/Santiment](https://www.cryptotimes.io/2026/10/06/santiment-challenges-ada-short-squeeze-narrative-as-open-interest-hits-304m/)). The best rigorous result is from the BIS and covers only BTC/ETH monthly: a 10% rise in standardised carry predicts short liquidations equal to **22% of OI** over the next month ([BIS WP 1087](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1087.pdf)). Order-book imbalance predicts Binance perp returns out of sample over 4–12 hours, but with R² of only **0.046–0.176%** ([arXiv 2607.09426](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.09426)). Kaiko's case studies show that thin books amplify moves. AEUR rose more than 300% while bid and ask depth fell 92–96% ([Kaiko](https://www.kaiko.com/resources/the-crypto-liquidity-concentration-report)). None of this has been tested on the question the user is asking. That means the ground is open, not that it is a dead end.

The data question is settled. A direct listing of the bucket behind data.binance.vision shows USD-M daily `metrics` files. Each holds 288 five-minute rows per day with `sum_open_interest` (in coin units), `sum_open_interest_value`, the top-trader account and position ratios, the global account long/short ratio, and the taker buy/sell volume ratio. The bucket also has `bookDepth` files: snapshots about every 30 seconds of cumulative depth and notional at ±0.2, 1, 2, 3, 4 and 5% from mid. For small caps, both start on the perp's listing date ([data.binance.vision metrics](https://data.binance.vision/?prefix=data/futures/um/daily/metrics/); [bookDepth](https://data.binance.vision/?prefix=data/futures/um/daily/bookDepth/)). The user confirmed coverage back to at least September 2024 for the event coins. The 30-day limit applies only to the REST endpoints ([Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/market-data/rest-api/Open-Interest-Statistics)). Some data is **not** free. There is no USD-M liquidation history (COIN-M's stopped in October 2024), and the live `forceOrder` stream sends only one liquidation per symbol per second ([Binance docs](https://developers.binance.com/docs/derivatives/usds-margined-futures/websocket-market-streams/All-Market-Liquidation-Order-Streams)). The `bookTicker` archive stopped in early 2024. Full L2 books, CoinGlass heatmaps, holder concentration and social-volume history are all paid. Three data-handling cautions: rows in the sample `metrics` file were out of order, so sort and check for gaps; the column-to-ratio mapping is inferred from names, so check it against the live REST values for a recent overlapping day; and USD OI rises mechanically with price, so **use coin-unit OI**.

These data make it possible to measure the positioning behind a pump, which the user's price/volume/funding/taker features could not. Two mechanisms work in opposite directions. A pure short-covering pump (OI falling, funding negative) burns its own fuel. A leveraged-long pump (OI rising, funding sharply positive) stacks liquidation fuel *below* the price. The hypothesis that best fits the case table is that runners are pumps where **coin-unit OI keeps rising while the premium or funding stays flat or negative and the retail long/short ratio falls**. In plain terms, new shorts keep arriving against the move and add to the squeeze. Funding *level* already failed. The new test is about *trajectories and interactions*: how ΔOI combines with the sign of the premium, and whether OI keeps building **after** the first high. That last measure is the free equivalent of the Moura lead-lag result. It asks whether participation was in place before the move and is now leaving, or is still arriving.

## Original angles worth trying, scored on novelty and testability

The user wants ideas that other traders are not already using. Some of the research suggestions have already been tested by the user under other names and are dropped here. Down-candle volume asymmetry is the wick/red-volume test. Time-of-day buckets and staircase/shape scores overlap the time-of-day and nearest-neighbour shape-matching tests. "Spot-led" and premium-index path overlap the spot-led checks. Top-N is a portfolio the user has run. Path signatures and Hurst exponents would be high-dimensional fits on a small sample, which is how ML already failed. The candidates below remain, and each runs on free historical data.

**Estimated short-liquidation fuel relative to ask depth.** CoinGlass-style heatmaps are proprietary model estimates built from OI changes and assumed leverage tiers ([CoinGlass](https://www.coinglass.com/vi/learn/coinglass-intro-en)). The user can build an open version. Treat each 5-minute rise in coin-unit OI as positions opened at that bar's VWAP. Split them long/short using the taker ratio and the change in the long/short ratio. Project liquidation prices at 5–50x, and remove positions as price crosses them. Then divide the estimated short notional within +X% above price by `bookDepth` ask notional to +X%. A high ratio means a small push sets off forced buying larger than the visible resting supply. That is the ALPACA mechanism expressed as a number. No published work tests it.

**Market-maker behaviour during the pump.** From `bookDepth`, compute whether total depth thinned or refilled during the move, whether bid depth built up under the price, and how many dollars it took to move price +5% compared with the hour's traded volume. One analyst argues that market makers step back after "death-trap" listings ([The Block](https://www.theblock.co/news/defi/2025-02-06-ai16z-and-vvv-see-double-digit-price-drops-since-death-trap-perps-listings-analyst-says-339220)). If bids are being withdrawn while price rises, the move is being handed to late buyers.

**Smart money against the crowd.** The top-trader *position* ratio moving short while the *global account* ratio moves long is a common practitioner signal, but no published test of it exists. It is free in `metrics`.

**Trade-size texture.** Taker-flow direction failed, but *who* is trading has not been tested. Cong et al. showed that genuine trading follows Benford's law, clusters at round sizes and has power-law tails, and that departures from these patterns identify fake volume ([arXiv 2108.10984](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2108.10984)). Leaked exchange data later confirmed that the Benford and clustering tests work ([DCU](https://doras.dcu.ie/32898/1/Direct%20evidence%20of%20bitcoin%20wash%20trading.pdf)). Applied to each event using aggTrades, the candidate features are Benford deviation against the coin's own prior week, the share of identical clip sizes repeating within 60 seconds (metronomic algorithmic buying), and **participation breadth** (growth in small-trade count and in the number of distinct sizes). The hypothesis: runners pull in a widening crowd, while fades are one algorithm followed by retail chasing. This is not the same as rush orders, which measure how bursty the buying is, not how varied the traders are.

**Exchange-event calendar.** Delisting announcements (days left until futures close), Binance switching a symbol's funding interval (visible from the timestamps in the monthly `fundingRate` files), and monitoring-tag rounds (which mostly bring declines ([crypto.news](https://crypto.news/binance-adds-monitoring-tag-to-move-following-market-maker-scandal-and-rebrand/))). The sample is small, but this is where the biggest documented 3-day runs happened, and the end date is known in advance.

**Displayed-#1 mechanics and attention saturation.** These are cheap to compute from klines and nobody has studied them. Did the coin become #1 *actively*, by rising past the previous leader, or *passively*, because the leader's 24h window rolled off? Is this its first time in the top 3 in 14 days, or a repeat appearance with less fresh attention? Abnormal attention predicts returns with only partial reversal ([IDEAS 2024](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/beexfi/v44y2024ics2214635024001060.html)), and past returns drive future attention ([IDEAS 2021](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finlet/v40y2021ics1544612320306590.html)). The repeat-appearance test overlaps somewhat with the user's coin "personality" test, so define it strictly by how recent the last appearance was.

**Venue of origin.** Did the move start on Bybit perps or in Korea? Binance leads BTC price discovery about 70% of the time ([summary](https://www.socialscience.international/almost-all-bitcoin-price-transmission-comes-from-binance)), but no altcoin study exists. Korean retail prices KRW markets with little arbitrage and accounts for about 85% of altcoin volume there ([4Pillars](https://research.4pillars.io/en/research/the-tale-of-the-tail-in-the-korea-cex)). The kimchi premium tends to peak at altcoin tops ([4Pillars](https://research.4pillars.io/en/data/content/koreans-buy-the-alt-top-not-the-bitcoin-top)), so a Korea-led flag could signal either persistence or exhaustion. Bybit's trade archive is free ([bybit-bulk-downloader](https://github.com/aoki-h-jp/bybit-bulk-downloader)). Upbit minute candles have to be paged through the REST API and cover only coins listed on Upbit; MYX and COAI were not.

**Payoff shape instead of prediction.** If no selection signal works, convexity can do part of the job. Pyramiding (enter with a third of the size and add at +20% and +40%), a delayed "still #1 after N hours" entry, volatility-scaled stops sized for constant dollar risk, and time stops all reshape returns without a classifier. Volatility management reduces crypto momentum crashes ([Vaasa](https://osuva.uwasa.fi/items/2b92e591-acb3-445f-b33d-1a943a8972f5/full)). Trend-followers attribute their edge to exits and sizing applied across many trends ([Man Group](https://www.man.com/insights/in-crypto-we-trend)). The one regime result with some support is that MAX momentum is strongest in market upturns ([Reading](https://reading-clone.eprints-hosting.org/99101/1/MAX%20Momentum.R1.pdf)), which justifies an alt-market regime gate. Rough arithmetic for pyramiding: if about 59% of entries never gain another 20%, those trades are stopped out on a one-third starter position, while runners carry full size. That changes the expected value even at AUC 0.5. Whether it beats the current ladder after fees and gap-downs is an empirical question.

### How to test without fooling yourself

The user has already tried about fifteen feature families, so the chance of a false positive is now the main danger. Each experiment should be **pre-registered**: one hypothesis, one direction, and at most two or three thresholds, fixed before looking at the data. Evaluate it on a chronological split, using roughly September 2024 to September 2025 to fit and October 2025 to September 2026 to test, plus a walk-forward check. With about 1,200 test events at a 41% base rate, the standard error of AUC is about 0.017. A real effect therefore needs an AUC of at least about 0.55 *and* a demand for more evidence because many tests have been run, which means a z-score above 3, not 2. Since "raises both tails" is the known failure mode, judge each feature on a **directional** target: P(+20% before −15%) and net P&L per trade using the bot's own exits. A higher P(+50%) alone does not count. A feature that only predicts larger variance should be moved to stop width and position size, not used for entry. A simple bound gives the size of the prize. Perfect selection earns about +15% per trade, and buying every #1 earns about zero, so even a filter that captures a tenth of that gap is worth around +1.5% per trade.

| 6 | **Exchange-event calendar**: days left until futures close after a delisting; switch to 1h funding; monitoring tag | Binance announcements, `fundingRate` timestamps | Delisting with high OI and more than 1 day left → run; funding switch → run under way; unlock or close within 24h → exit | Medium; small N, very large payoffs |

| 9 | **Displayed-#1 mechanics**: became #1 actively or passively; first appearance or repeat within 14 days | 1m klines, all symbols | Active, first appearance → run | Medium; ranks for the whole universe must be rebuilt |

Not recommended: Google Trends (no reliable intraday history), social volume and holder concentration (paid or not historical), DEX-origin tracing (impractical on a free server), path signatures and Hurst exponents (too many dimensions for the sample size).

## Conclusion

The repeated failures carry a clear message. Anything visible on the price chart at the moment a coin reaches #1 is already reflected in the price and is spread evenly over runners and duds, because the same attention drives both. The case evidence suggests the difference lies in a variable the chart cannot show: **how much forced buying remains**. That covers shorts still trapped, a float still cornered, and a fixed date that ends the game. Such variables become measurable only with positioning and order-book data, and those data have only now been confirmed free for the whole test window. If the top three experiments come out flat as well, under a strict pre-registered, out-of-sample test with a directional target, that is strong evidence that selection on this event cannot be predicted from public data. Effort should then move entirely to payoff shape (experiment 7) and to forward collection of the data that cannot be downloaded historically (the liquidation stream, social timing). In that case, any edge would come from data the user collects live, not from history.

A second point follows from the research. The one discriminator with published support is about **timing relative to the peak**, not about levels at the signal. That favours designs that wait for the market to reveal its type, such as checking whether flow keeps arriving after the first high, adding to the position only after confirmation, or entering only if the coin is still #1 after some hours. The cost is giving up some of the early move. Given that perfect selection is worth about +15% per trade and blind entry about zero, a later entry that is better informed is probably the most realistic way to get part of that gap.
